Cue03

Footstep-safe

Music stays out of the footstep band.

One switch cuts the music by at least 6 dB around 3.2 kHz, where footsteps live, rolls off the highs, lowers the bass and makes ducking competitive. The cues you play by stay clear.

Cue04

Ducking

Music steps back when the game gets loud.

Sidestage listens to everything except the music to decide. Choose how hard: Off, Gentle, Standard or Competitive.

OffGentleStandardCompetitive

Cue05

No double audio

You never hear an app twice.

The app's normal sound is hidden automatically while Sidestage plays it in 3D. If Sidestage is killed, your audio comes back: a helper process restores it on Windows and Linux, and macOS does it by itself. sidestage restore is there if you ever need it.

Cue06

Privacy

Nothing leaves your machine.

There is no network code in Sidestage at all. No telemetry, no accounts, no update checks. Audio is processed in memory and never recorded.

05 · Questions

Before the curtain goes up.

Or build it yourself with Rust 1.95 or newer:

$cargo run --release
Does it work on speakers?

No. Sidestage renders binaural audio, which needs one channel per ear. On speakers the 3D effect falls apart.

How many apps can it place at once?

Version 0.1 places one. The next update places up to 8 at once, each with its own position and preset, plus voice presets for Discord.

Up to 8 at once, each with its own position and preset. Voice chat gets its own presets, tuned for Discord.

Does Sidestage touch my game?

Never. It captures only the apps you choose. The game's audio is not captured, routed or processed, so its footsteps and gunshots keep their exact position and timing.

Why does the Windows Volume mixer show my apps at 0?

That is how the originals are hidden on Windows: each source's app is turned down to -120 dB and its captured copy is boosted back by the same amount. Use Sidestage's own volume sliders instead. If Sidestage is killed, a helper process puts the volumes back right away.

The music sounds in front of me, not behind.

Generic head models sometimes confuse front and back. Moving the music a little to one side, or adding distance and room, helps. On Windows, also turn off spatial sound (Windows Sonic, Dolby Atmos or DTS) for your headphones.

My Bluetooth headset loses the effect.

A headset in hands-free (microphone) mode switches to low-quality mono, which can't carry 3D audio. Use its stereo mode. On Windows, Sidestage warns you about this.

What does it keep on my computer?

Only its settings, its logs and small crash-recovery journals that record which app volumes or routes to put back. Audio is processed in memory and never saved, and nothing is sent anywhere.
