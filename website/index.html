Scene iii

Footstep-safe

One switch cuts the music by at least 6 dB around 3.2 kHz, where footsteps live, rolls off the highs, lowers the bass and switches ducking to Competitive. The sound you listen for gets room to cut through.

Scene iv

Ducking

When the game gets loud, the music steps back, then returns. Sidestage listens to everything except the music to decide. Pick how hard it ducks, from Off to Competitive.

  • Off
  • Gentle
  • Standard
  • Competitive

Scene v

Heard once, never twice

The app's normal sound is hidden automatically, so you only hear the 3D version. If Sidestage is killed, your audio comes back: a helper process restores it on Windows and Linux, and macOS does it by itself. Every start also checks for anything an earlier run left behind.

  • Spotify, originalhidden
  • Spotify, in 3Dplaying
  • After a crashput back

$ sidestage restore # by hand, if you ever need it

Scene vi

Nothing leaves your machine

Sidestage has no network code at all. Audio is processed in memory and never recorded. What you listen to stays between you and your headphones.

Front of house

Questions at the door.

Or build it yourself with Rust 1.95 or newer:

$ cargo run --release

Does it work on speakers?

No. Sidestage renders binaural audio, which needs one channel per ear. On speakers the 3D effect falls apart.

Does Sidestage touch my game?

Never. It captures only the apps you choose. The game's audio is not captured, routed or processed, so its footsteps and gunshots keep their exact position and timing.

Why does the Windows Volume mixer show my music app at 0?

That is how the original is hidden on Windows: the app is turned down to -120 dB and the captured copy is boosted back by the same amount. Use Sidestage's own volume slider instead. If Sidestage is killed, a helper process puts the app's volume back right away.

The music sounds in front of me, not behind.

Generic head models sometimes confuse front and back. Moving the music a little to one side, or adding distance and room, helps. On Windows, also turn off spatial sound (Windows Sonic, Dolby Atmos or DTS) for your headphones.

My Bluetooth headset loses the effect.

A headset in hands-free (microphone) mode switches to low-quality mono, which can't carry 3D audio. Use its stereo mode. On Windows, Sidestage warns you about this.

What does it keep on my computer?

Only its settings, its logs and small crash-recovery journals that record which app volumes or routes to put back. Audio is processed in memory and never saved, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Curtain. Enjoy the game.
